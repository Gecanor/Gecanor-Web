**Selling a cosmetic in Spain and the rest of the EU does not require prior authorisation for each product, but it does require five things before the first sale: a responsible person, a safety assessment, the Product Information File (PIF), notification on the CPNP portal and a compliant label.** They all come from [Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2009/1223/oj/eng); in Spain it is complemented by [Royal Decree 85/2018](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2018/02/23/85).

## 1. The responsible person

Every cosmetic sold in the EU must have a responsible person, a natural or legal person established in the EU. They guarantee that the product complies with the regulation and their details appear on the label. If you sell under your own brand, this is usually your company; if you import from outside the EU, it is the importer, who can appoint a third party in writing.

## 2. The safety assessment (CPSR)

Before it is placed on the market, the product must undergo a safety assessment that results in the Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR), in two parts: part A gathers the safety information (composition, stability, microbiology, impurities, packaging material, toxicological profile of the ingredients…) and part B is the assessor's evaluation and conclusion. It is signed by a professional with the qualifications the regulation requires. Products intended for children under 3 or for external intimate hygiene need a specific assessment.

The report draws on tests such as stability, packaging compatibility, microbiological analysis, the preservative challenge test and, depending on the product, skin tolerance tests.

## 3. The Product Information File (PIF)

The PIF is the technical dossier that the responsible person keeps available to the authorities at the address shown on the label. It includes:

- The product description.
- The safety report (CPSR).
- The manufacturing method and a statement of compliance with good manufacturing practice (ISO 22716).
- Proof of the effects claimed.
- Data on any animal testing related to the product or its ingredients.

It is kept for 10 years after the last batch is placed on the market and updated whenever the formula, packaging or data change.

## 4. Notification on the CPNP portal

Before the first sale, the responsible person notifies the product on the CPNP, the European Commission's [free notification portal](https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/29563/attachments/1/translations/en/renditions/pdf). A single notification covers the whole EU. Notifying is not an approval: compliance remains the responsibility of whoever places the product on the market.

## 5. The label

The packaging must show, among other things: the name and address of the responsible person, nominal content, the date of minimum durability if it is under 30 months or, otherwise, the period after opening (PAO), precautions for use, batch number, the product's function if not obvious from its presentation, and the ingredient list. In Spain, in Spanish.

## What about the AEMPS?

In Spain, anyone who physically manufactures, fills or labels cosmetics, or imports them from outside the EU, must file a [statement of activities with the AEMPS](https://www.aemps.gob.es/preguntas-y-respuestas-mas-frecuentes-sobre-la-declaracion-responsable-de-actividades-de-fabricacion-y-o-importacion-de-productos-cosmeticos/) before starting (Royal Decree 85/2018). If your brand outsources manufacturing to a laboratory, the laboratory holds it: check before you sign. Serious undesirable effects must also be reported to the authorities (cosmetovigilance).

## Mistakes we often see

- Starting to sell before the CPNP notification is done.
- Changing packaging without updating the compatibility test and the PIF.
- Labels with claims ("erases wrinkles", "cures") that cannot be substantiated or that turn the product into a medicine.
- Not knowing who the responsible person is when the brand and the manufacturer are different companies.
