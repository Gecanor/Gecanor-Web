**Manufacturing your own cosmetics brand involves six stages: briefing, formulation and samples, stability and compatibility testing, safety and documentation, packaging, and production in a laboratory. With a white label formula you can launch in weeks and with a small run (at Gecanor, from 500 units per product); with a custom formula it takes around three months, because the tests have durations that cannot be shortened.**

It is the question we hear most often in a first meeting. The short answer is "it depends"; the useful one is knowing what it depends on and which part of the timeline is in your hands.

## How long does it take to manufacture a cosmetics brand?

As a guide, based on our experience coordinating projects with Spanish contract manufacturing laboratories:

| Starting point | Indicative time until the product is ready to sell |
| --- | --- |
| White label formula with stock packaging | About 7 weeks |
| White label formula with custom packaging | 2 to 4 months |
| Custom formula with stock packaging | About 3 months |

These timelines are for a product without special claims. Each stage explained below can make them longer or shorter.

## Can you manufacture a cosmetics brand with a small minimum order?

Yes. You do not need your own laboratory or to produce thousands of units: manufacturing is outsourced to an authorised contract manufacturing laboratory, which produces, fills and labels the product under your brand. What sets the real minimum is usually the packaging rather than the formula: stock packaging allows short runs, while a custom container or colour can require thousands of units from the supplier. At Gecanor we work with Spanish laboratories and the minimum order starts at 500 units per product, so you can test your brand on the market before producing more.

## Stage 1. Briefing and feasibility

Everything starts with a document stating what product you want, who it is for, what it should promise, where it will be sold and at what price. With that, the formulation team can tell you whether it is feasible, which actives make sense and what unit cost to expect. A clear brief saves weeks: changing the concept halfway through development means repeating samples and tests.

## Stage 2. Formulation and samples

The laboratory prepares a first lab-scale sample and adjusts it based on your feedback on texture, scent, colour and skin feel. Several rounds are normal. Here the timeline depends mostly on you: the sooner you approve each sample and the less the brief changes, the sooner the formula is final.

## Stage 3. Stability, compatibility and preservation

Once the formula is final, the tests begin, and they cannot be sped up beyond what their methods require:

- **Stability**: checks that the product keeps its appearance, scent, pH and viscosity under different temperature and light conditions. Since waiting for the whole shelf life to pass is not practical, accelerated tests are used to predict it, following the [Cosmetics Europe](https://www.cosmeticseurope.eu/files/5914/6407/8121/Guidelines_on_Stability_Testing_of_Cosmetics_CE-CTFA_-_2004.pdf) guidelines.
- **Packaging compatibility**: the formula is tested in its final container to rule out interactions. If you change the container, it has to be repeated.
- **Challenge test** (preservative efficacy): the product is deliberately contaminated and its response is measured. Under the ISO 11930 method, counts are taken at 7, 14 and 28 days, so this test takes at least four weeks.

This stage carries the most weight in a custom development. With white label, the formula already has these studies.

## Stage 4. Safety and documentation

With the results, a qualified assessor signs the **safety assessment** and the **product information file (PIF)** required by [Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2009/1223/oj/eng) is completed. If the product needs it, a patch test on volunteers is also carried out to confirm skin compatibility. Be careful with claims: to say something like "suitable for sensitive skin" on the label you need a study that supports it, and some, such as the human repeat insult patch test (HRIPT), take about six weeks.

## Stage 5. Packaging and label

This stage runs in parallel with the others and is often the one that ends up setting the date. Custom containers and boxes have their own production lead times and minimum orders, and the label cannot be finalised until the ingredient list, the responsible person and the date of minimum durability or period after opening (PAO), which comes from the stability tests, are confirmed. That is why it pays to choose the packaging early: changing it at the end means repeating the compatibility test.

## Stage 6. CPNP notification and production

Before it is sold, the product is notified on the EU CPNP portal. At the same time, production is scheduled at the laboratory, and the product is filled, labelled and the batch released with its quality documentation. We explain this step by step in [Legal requirements to sell cosmetics in the EU](/resources/requisitos-legales-cosmeticos-ue).

## How to shorten your cosmetics brand timeline

| Decision | Effect on the timeline |
| --- | --- |
| Start from a white label formula | Removes formulation and most tests |
| Final brief from the start | Fewer sample rounds |
| Choose the packaging before the formula is final | Avoids repeating the compatibility test |
| Stock packaging instead of custom | Shorter supplier lead time and lower minimum order |
| Simple claims | Fewer efficacy studies |

Many cosmetics brands launch first with white label products and small runs to get to market quickly, while developing their hero product as a custom formula in parallel.
