How to launch a food supplement brand in Spain

By David Castillo, CEO, Gecanor Beauty Tech · Published on 26 November 2026

To sell food supplements under your own brand in Spain you need four things: your company registered in the food health register, each product notified to the health authority of your autonomous community before or at the same time as its first sale, a composition with permitted ingredients, and a label that complies with food law, using only authorised health claims. The rules are not the same as for cosmetics, even though many beauty brands launch both lines.

1. Food supplement, supplement or nutricosmetics: it is the same thing

In Spain the legal name is "complemento alimenticio" (food supplement), although the market also talks about supplements or nutricosmetics (supplements aimed at skin, hair or nails). They are foods, not medicines: they supplement the diet and cannot be presented as a treatment for any disease.

2. Register your company in the food health register

The company that places the product on the market must be registered in the General Health Register of Food Businesses and Foods (RGSEAA), under Royal Decree 191/2011. Registration goes through your autonomous community; in Catalonia, together with the regional register (RSIPAC). If you outsource manufacturing, the manufacturer has its own registration, and your company needs its own as the business responsible for marketing the product.

3. Notify each product before selling it

Royal Decree 1487/2009 requires the notification of placing on the market of each supplement before or at the same time as its first sale. It is made by the first business to place the product on the Spanish market, whether manufacturer, importer, distributor or retailer, to the authority of the autonomous community where it has its registered office. It is submitted with the label and a fee. Once processed, the product appears in the public RGSEAA database.

As with cosmetics, notifying is not an approval: the company remains responsible for the product being safe and legal.

4. Check the composition

Vitamins and minerals, their forms and other substances with a nutritional or physiological effect must match the lists in Royal Decree 1487/2009 (amended by Royal Decree 130/2018). If an ingredient is not on those lists but the product is lawfully sold in another EU country, it may rely on mutual recognition. Ingredients considered novel foods need EU authorisation.

5. Prepare the label and the claims

The label follows Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 on food information plus the specific particulars for supplements: the name "food supplement", the nutrients or substances that characterise it, the recommended daily dose, a warning not to exceed it, a statement that it does not replace a varied diet and that it should be kept out of the reach of children.

Benefit statements ("contributes to the maintenance of normal hair") are health claims and may only be used if they are authorised on the EU list (Regulation 432/2012, which contains 222 claims) and under their conditions of use. This is where most brands go wrong, including on social media and on their website.

6. Manufacture and plan the launch

Supplements are made in formats such as capsules, tablets, powder, sticks or gummies, and the format shapes the formula, the packaging and the minimum order. Build the registration and notification steps into your timeline: without the company's health registration you cannot notify.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need health registration to sell supplements under my own brand?

Yes. The company that markets them must be registered in the RGSEAA, even if it does not manufacture.

Where is a food supplement notified?

With the health authority of the autonomous community where the business responsible for first placing it on the market has its registered office; in Catalonia, the Catalan Public Health Agency.

Do I have to wait for an answer before selling?

No: the notification is made before or at the same time as the first sale. Even so, the authority may ask for changes later.

Can I say my supplement improves the skin?

Only with claims authorised on the EU list and under their exact conditions. Free wording or references to curing or preventing disease are prohibited.

Do the same rules apply as for cosmetics?

No. Supplements are foods and follow food law (Royal Decree 1487/2009, Regulations 1169/2011 and 1924/2006); cosmetics follow Regulation 1223/2009.
